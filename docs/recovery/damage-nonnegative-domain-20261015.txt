Why broader admission needs no new arithmetic:
The existing comparisons select max(pair), the existing >1 ternary implements
min(1,max), and existing volatile-float sub computes 1 minus that bounded value.
These operations and their per-bank order are unchanged. Any newly admitted
finite value above1 makes its bank maximum above1; MIN selects exactly1 before
subtraction. Even the largest finite input never reaches arithmetic with a
large operand, so this extension introduces no overflow or narrowing conversion.
The other bank follows its unchanged existing computation. Thus the new region
produces numeric health0 for that bank, while retaining the leaf[0,1] checks.

Signed zero and rounding scope:
The existing v<0 guard admits -0 as numeric nonnegative, as it already did.
Equal MAX/MIN operands may have implementation-specific source signed-zero tie
selection; either sign of a zero damage maximum gives exactly numeric health1.
For newly admitted >1 pairs the MIN result is positive exact1; 1-1 is numeric0.
Its zero sign can depend on the floating-point rounding environment (round-down
may yield -0). The delta neither sets the environment nor canonicalizes that
sign, and makes no new bitwise signed-zero parity claim. The leaf comparisons
and nonnegative segment conversion accept either zero sign. Under the existing
intended ordinary binary32 environment, the added region requires only exact
selection and exact cancellation. Existing subnormal/host FP-mode/EE rounding
equivalence for the old [0,1] region remains unproved by this source-only change.
No new std::min/std::max, fmin/fmax, epsilon, cast, fused operation, clamp or
arithmetic helper was introduced. NaN/inf/negative EE semantics remain closed.
